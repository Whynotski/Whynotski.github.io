Always learning, so I always have something worth teaching.

I chase new things — technology, skills, ideas — and I'm at my best handing them to someone else. Skis, a swim stroke, a boat that finally clicks as a crew. I know it landed when we're laughing.

About

I spent 27 years in federal science: first as a chemist and science policy analyst at the FDA, then as a data analyst at NIH and an innovator at the HHS Idea Lab. The thread through all of it was learning how a complicated system works, then making it clearer for the people who rely on it.

These days I put that energy into learning new things, especially AI and web technology, and teaching them to whoever wants to come along: on ski hills, at the pool, and in Python classes.

Chemistry

Where I first learned that explaining something well is the surest test of whether I understand it.

Sailing

Crewing, and building crews

A boat only goes fast when the crew moves as one. I like the slow work of getting a group of individuals to that point, and the payoff when it clicks under real pressure.

Biking, kayaking, hiking

Just for me, and for whoever's around

Not every outdoor thing has to be a lesson. Some of it is just moving through the world, which turns out to be its own kind of recharge.

The signal

I've had hearing problems since I was a kid, which means connecting with people has always taken a little extra work: reading the room, filling gaps, staying in sync.

So when a conversation gets to genuine, shared laughter, that's not a small thing to me. It's the clearest proof I have that the connection actually happened.
